WE THE PEOPLE

A Defense of the Capitol

Dusk over the National Mall. A shambling crowd pours across the lawn toward the Capitol steps — vacant-eyed, some in red caps, a few waving blue banners, all of them past the point of listening. You are unarmed but for two pieces of paper and the flag.

Read to them. A well-thrown founding document is enough to snap a citizen out of it — the cap falls, the flag drops, and they wander home. When they get close, wave Old Glory: a few good sweeps will turn back anyone in reach. Walk over the caps and banners they leave behind to collect them — but they don't lie there long, and the clean-up gets quicker every wave.

Every five minutes the Shaman comes up the Mall in his buffalo headdress. One page won't reach him. Keep reading until the headdress comes off.
